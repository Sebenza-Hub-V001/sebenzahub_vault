# Chapter 20 — Autopilot and auto-apply safeguards

Autopilot is the set of automation toggles that let Sebenza Hub act on your behalf — drafting cover letters, sending follow-ups, researching companies, even auto-tailoring your CV. It's the most powerful feature in this guide and the easiest one to misuse. Set it up carefully and it saves hours a week. Set it up badly and it scales a bad pipeline as fast as a good one.

This chapter walks you through where Autopilot lives, exactly which toggles you'll find, and the principles for setting them up safely.

## Where Autopilot lives

Open **Settings** (`/dashboard/individual/settings`) and switch to the **Automation** tab. You'll find three sections:

1. **Autopilot** — the application-flow automations covered in this chapter.
2. **Interview AI** — auto-research, auto-prep questions, and auto-thank-you-note for booked interviews (see Chapter 14).
3. **Market Radar** — passive watches on companies, roles, and industries (see Chapter 22).

![[i-ch20-autopilot-settings.png]]

This chapter focuses on the first section. Chapters 13 and 22 cover the others.

## What Autopilot can do

The Autopilot section has six switches plus a weekly volume target. Each one is independently togglable — you don't have to enable Autopilot wholesale; you can pick the one or two automations that fit your situation and leave the rest off.

| Toggle | What it does |
|--------|--------------|
| **Auto CV Tailoring** | When you apply to a job, the platform automatically picks (or lightly edits) the most relevant CV instead of using your default. |
| **Auto Cover Letter** | The platform drafts a cover letter for every application using your profile and the JD. You can pick the **cover letter tone** (professional / conversational / enthusiastic) for the autogenerated drafts. |
| **Auto Follow-Up** | If an application has had no response for the configured **Follow-Up Days** (default 7), the platform sends a polite follow-up. **Follow-Up Max Attempts** (default 2) caps how many times this fires before the platform stops chasing. |
| **Auto Thank-You** | After an interview, a thank-you note is drafted and sent on your behalf. (The platform also has a richer Auto Thank-You Note option in the Interview AI section — see Chapter 14.) |
| **Auto Company Research** | When you apply, the platform pre-pulls the AI Company Research report and attaches it to the application. Default: on. |
| **Auto-Decline Below** | An optional match-score threshold under which the platform auto-declines or skips matches without asking. Leave this empty unless you've validated your matching at length. |

There's also a **Weekly Application Target** slider — the volume Autopilot will aim for in a typical week. The default is 10. Lower it to 5–7 if you're employed; raise it to 15–20 if you're between jobs and your matching is well-tuned.

A **Pause Until** field lets you pause every Autopilot automation for a set date if you're going on leave or a holiday.

## Why you should be careful

The risks specifically:

| Risk | What it looks like |
|------|---------------------|
| Wrong-fit applications | The platform auto-applies to roles that don't actually match your real preferences |
| Off-brand cover letters | The cover letter says something about you you wouldn't have said |
| Burst patterns | Twenty applications in five minutes — recruiters notice this |
| Ghost-job applications | Auto-apply on flagged ghost listings because filters didn't catch them |
| Sub-quality CV selection | The wrong CV version attached to a role that needed a tailored one |
| Loss of mental tracking | You stop knowing where you've applied, which makes follow-up impossible |

The reason is structural: the value of an application is not its existence — it's your *attention* to it. A hand-written cover letter referencing a specific JD detail is signal. A generic AI-generated cover letter on a wrong-fit job is noise. Autopilot can scale either, depending on how it's tuned.

## The principles to apply

Whichever automations you turn on, these principles hold up:

1. **Tune Career DNA and Opportunity Weights first.** [[01 How-To Documents/individual/21-career-dna|Chapter 21]] sets up the matching logic Autopilot relies on. If matching is bad, Autopilot will scale the bad matches — fix the inputs before you turn on the outputs.
2. **Start with one toggle, not all six.** Turn on **Auto Company Research** and **Auto Cover Letter** first — they make manual applications faster without doing anything irreversible. Once you trust the cover letters, then think about turning on follow-ups.
3. **Don't enable Auto-Decline Below unless you're sure.** This toggle is the only one that *removes* applications from your queue without asking. Leave the field empty unless you've spent weeks watching how the match scores correlate with your actual interest.
4. **Cap the weekly volume.** Even on a serious search, more than 15–20 applications a week through Autopilot starts producing noise. Use the slider.
5. **Review the queue daily.** Whatever Autopilot has done, look at it. Application Tracker (Chapter 13) shows everything in one place. If you wouldn't have sent something it sent, tighten the rule.
6. **Don't run Auto Cover Letter on senior or specialised roles.** These need a human voice. For those, write the cover letter yourself even if the rest of the flow is automated.
7. **Treat Auto Follow-Up cautiously.** Set Follow-Up Days to at least 7 and Max Attempts to 1 or 2. Generic "checking in" messages from your account, in volume, can damage your reputation with recruiters. One follow-up per application, well-spaced, is the maximum acceptable cadence.
8. **Pause Autopilot when you're not paying attention.** Going on leave? Use the Pause Until field. Better than waking up to 40 auto-applications you don't remember.

## When Autopilot makes sense

There are situations where Autopilot fits the search:

- **You're applying to high-volume, standardised roles** where customisation barely matters (entry-level, retail, contact centre)
- **You're searching while employed** and don't have time for daily manual application sessions
- **You've already validated your targeting and CV** in Part 5 and are confident the inputs are good
- **You're in a thin market** where seeing every new listing fast is more valuable than applying with care

## When Autopilot is the wrong tool

- **You're early in your search** and still figuring out what works
- **You're applying to senior, specialised, or rare roles** where each application is a real conversation
- **Your profile or CV needs more work** (Part 2 isn't done)
- **You haven't identified your direction yet** (Chapter 18)
- **You haven't validated any responses on manual applications** — Autopilot will scale a bad pipeline as fast as a good one

## A reasonable starter configuration

If you want a starting point and you're a few weeks into a search that's working manually:

| Toggle | Suggested setting |
|--------|-------------------|
| Auto CV Tailoring | Off (start by picking the CV yourself) |
| Auto Cover Letter | On, tone: conversational |
| Auto Follow-Up | On, days: 10, max attempts: 2 |
| Auto Thank-You | Off (write these yourself for the first few interviews) |
| Auto Company Research | On |
| Auto-Decline Below | Empty |
| Weekly Application Target | 10 |

Run this for two weeks. Check the queue daily. If the cover letters are good and the follow-ups are sensible, increase the weekly target. If the cover letters are off-brand, lower the tone or turn the toggle off and write them yourself.

## Auditing what Autopilot has done

![[i-ch20-auto-applied-review-queue.png]]

Open **My Applications** (Chapter 13) at least once a day while Autopilot is running. Look for:

- Applications you don't remember submitting — read the cover letter the platform sent
- Applications to companies that look low-quality — tighten your filters
- Follow-up messages logged in the Inbox — read the message it sent on your behalf
- Match scores below your usual floor — check that Auto-Decline isn't turned on

If anything looks wrong, **switch the offending toggle off immediately**, fix the underlying setting (Career DNA, target role, weekly target), and only turn it back on after a week of clean manual applications.

## Checklist

- [ ] You understand that Autopilot is high-risk if misused
- [ ] You've finished Career DNA and Opportunity Weights (Chapter 21) before enabling anything
- [ ] You've enabled at most one or two automations to start
- [ ] You've capped Follow-Up attempts and Weekly Target at sensible numbers
- [ ] You've set up a daily review ritual for actions Autopilot took
- [ ] You know how to pause Autopilot before going on leave

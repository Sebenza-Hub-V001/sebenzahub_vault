# Chapter 22 — Market Radar

Market Radar is a passive-monitoring feature. Instead of you going out to look at the market, the platform watches it on your behalf and tells you when something specific you care about happens. It's the most useful feature for people who *aren't* actively job-hunting — and surprisingly useful for active hunters who want to keep an eye on niches they don't want to forget about.

Market Radar lives inside **Settings → Automation tab → Market Radar** sub-section. It's not a main dashboard page; it's a configuration that quietly produces alerts.

## What you can watch

Market Radar lets you set watches on three things plus four alert categories:

| Watch | What it tracks |
|-------|----------------|
| **Watched Companies** | Specific employers — when they post new roles, hire, expand, or change leadership |
| **Watched Roles** | Specific job titles — when new postings appear |
| **Watched Industries** | Whole sectors — when there's significant hiring movement |

In addition to those three watch lists, you toggle the kinds of alerts the radar will produce:

| Alert toggle | What it sends |
|--------------|---------------|
| **Salary Trend Alerts** | Significant movement in pay for the roles or industries you're watching |
| **Hiring Burst Alerts** | A watched company or industry suddenly opening multiple roles at once |
| **Layoff Alerts** | Reductions or restructurings in watched companies |
| **Competitor Tracking** | Movement at companies you've flagged as competitors to your current or recent employer |

Finally, set the **Alert Frequency** — daily, weekly, or monthly digest.

## Why it matters

Most job hunting is reactive: you decide you need to look, you go look, and you discover a market that's been moving for months without you. Market Radar changes that — by the time you decide to look, the alerts have already been quietly accumulating, and you have months of data on which companies are hiring, which roles are appearing, and how compensation in your space has been moving.

Three real use cases:

### Use case 1 — You're employed and not looking, but want to stay informed

Set Market Radar watches on:

- **2–3 specific companies** you'd consider working for (Watched Companies)
- **Your current role title** (Watched Roles), paired with **Salary Trend Alerts** on, so you see what the market is paying

Frequency: weekly digest. Volume: low. You're not job hunting — you're staying informed.

The first time one of your watch-companies posts a role you'd genuinely take, you have a head start over everyone who only looked when they decided to look.

### Use case 2 — You're actively looking but want to keep niche options alive

You're applying for one main type of role, but there's a secondary niche you'd jump on if it appeared. Add the niche role title to **Watched Roles** and forget about it. Don't waste daily attention scrolling for it — let the radar catch it.

Frequency: daily for the rare-niche watch only; weekly for everything else.

### Use case 3 — You're researching the market for a future move

You're not moving for 12 months, but you want to understand where to position yourself. Set Market Radar watches on:

- **The companies in that space** (Watched Companies) — to see who's growing
- **The role you might move into** (Watched Roles) — to see how often it appears
- **The industry that role sits in** (Watched Industries) — to see whether the wider sector is expanding or contracting
- Toggle on **Salary Trend Alerts** and **Hiring Burst Alerts** so you see the meaningful movement

You'll have a year of data when you actually start looking, and that data will shape better decisions than a panic search would.

## How to set it up

Open **Settings** → **Automation** tab and scroll to the **Market Radar** sub-section.

1. **Toggle Market Radar on.**
2. **Add to Watched Companies** — type the company name and confirm. Repeat for 2–3.
3. **Add to Watched Roles** — type the role title (e.g. *Senior Backend Engineer*) and confirm.
4. **Add to Watched Industries** — pick from the list or type a custom industry.
5. **Pick which alert types to receive** — Salary Trend, Hiring Burst, Layoff, Competitor Tracking.
6. **Set the Alert Frequency** — daily, weekly, or monthly.

Save. The radar starts producing alerts on the cadence you chose.

## Don't over-watch

The temptation with Market Radar is to set 20 watches and feel comprehensive. Don't. Two principles:

1. **Three to five entries across all three watch lists is the sweet spot.** More than that and you stop reading the alerts.
2. **Use weekly as the default frequency.** Daily is rarely needed; monthly is too sparse to act on.

If your alert volume is more than you'd reasonably read in five minutes a week, you've over-watched. Trim.

## Reviewing what the radar surfaces

Once a week (or whenever your digest lands), spend five minutes on it:

- **Read the alerts.** Don't just glance.
- **Note what's interesting.** A company you didn't know was hiring, a salary trend you didn't expect.
- **Take action only if something is genuinely urgent.** Most weeks the right action is "nothing — but I now know more than I did".
- **Periodically tune.** If a watch keeps producing irrelevant alerts, refine the entry or remove it.

## Market Radar vs Saved Searches

These two features overlap. The difference:

| Feature | Best for | What it watches |
|---------|----------|------------------|
| **Saved Search** (Chapter 10) | Active job hunters | New job listings matching your criteria |
| **Market Radar** | Passive observers, future planners | Broader signals — companies, hiring trends, salary movement, layoffs, competitor moves |

If you're actively applying, Saved Searches are your primary tool. If you're not, Market Radar is. They can coexist — Saved Searches for "is anything new I should apply to?" and Market Radar for "is the world I might want to enter changing?"

## Pair Market Radar with Career Pulse

Market Radar tells you what's moving *out there*. Career Pulse tells you where *you* stand inside that movement. Use them together — radar for incoming signals, pulse for the periodic check-up.

### Career Pulse Report — _Tier: see Pricing_

**What this feature is.** A personalised market report covering demand trend for your role, salary trajectory, competing-candidate density, hottest cities, and a set of recommended actions. It's diagnostic — it tells you where you stand, not what to do.

**Why it matters.** Mid-career professionals need to know which direction their market is going, not just where their next job is. Career Pulse is the analysis senior talent will pay for — it's the difference between guessing your market is healthy and knowing demand for your skills has been declining for two years.

**How to use it.**

1. Open **Career Pulse** (`/dashboard/individual/career-pulse`). Generate a fresh report.

![[i-ch22-career-pulse-report.png]]

2. Read it slowly — every section is structured to surface a specific blind spot.

![[i-ch22-demand-salary-trend.png]]

3. Cross-reference with what your Market Radar has been telling you. If the two disagree, the disagreement is the most useful signal in either report.
4. Re-generate quarterly, or after any major shift in your situation.

A worked example: you might believe your skills are in demand, but Career Pulse shows demand has been declining for two years. That's something to factor into the next direction decision (Chapter 18) and into the Career DNA tune-up (Chapter 21).

![[i-ch22-hottest-cities-heatmap.png]]

## When to turn Market Radar off

- When you've taken a new job and need to step away from the platform completely
- When the volume is overwhelming you (often a sign of over-watching, but sometimes a sign that this isn't the right tool for your situation)
- When you've finished researching the market and you're ready to actually apply

It can always be turned back on. Don't treat the configuration as permanent.

## Checklist

- [ ] You know which use case applies to you (employed/looking/researching)
- [ ] Set 3–5 entries across the three watch lists, no more
- [ ] Set the Alert Frequency to weekly (daily only for one or two genuinely time-sensitive watches)
- [ ] You have a weekly review ritual for the alerts
- [ ] You're prepared to tune watches that produce noise
- [ ] Generated a Career Pulse Report and cross-referenced it with the radar signals
